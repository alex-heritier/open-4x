# Starter asset provenance

PNG files under `packs/base/` are generated by the original Python pipeline in `art/` (procedural SVG scenes rasterised with `rsvg-convert`, then painterly finishing in NumPy). Regenerate with `cd art && .venv/bin/python build.py`; see `art/README.md`. `audio/bell.wav` and the combat sound effects (`volley`, `charge`, `gun`, `broadside`, `hit`, `fall`, `sink`, `yield`) are synthesized by `tools/asset-forge/` (`cargo run -p asset-forge`) from noise, filtered tones and envelopes written for this project, and combat motion and effects are procedural, so fights use no sampled sound, frame art, or animation file. The behaviour of fights on screen was specified from observation in `docs/combat-animation.md` and implemented from that text. Neither generator reads either reference image, the Civ3 installation, or any open-civ3 asset.

The two reference images in the workspace root are user-supplied inspiration and are not bundled into the game or web distribution. Terrain geometry follows the documented Civ3 tiling layout. The art direction (palette, lighting, painterly finish) was matched to the references by eye; every shape and texture is authored in code, and JSON definitions and Lua scripts are independent.

`packs/base/scenarios/world-1876.json` is generated by `tools/scenario-forge/` (`cargo run --release -p scenario-forge -- build`). Who held what on 1 January 1876, the cities, armies, and wars are original curated tables in `tools/scenario-forge/data/`. The geography comes from three public sources, none of which is redistributed: the coastline, lake, river, and modern country outlines are [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) 1:50m vector data (v5.1.2, public domain); land elevation and sea depth are NOAA's ETOPO5 5-arc-minute grid (National Geophysical Data Center, public domain); and the climate classes that decide each tile's base terrain and vegetation are the Köppen–Geiger map of Kottek, Grieser, Beck, Rudolf and Rubel, "World Map of the Köppen-Geiger climate classification updated", *Meteorologische Zeitschrift* 15 (2006) 259-263, 0.5° data from [koeppen-geiger.vu-wien.ac.at](https://koeppen-geiger.vu-wien.ac.at/), used here with attribution. The forge downloads those files once, checks them against pinned SHA-256 digests, and keeps them in the git-ignored `tools/scenario-forge/cache/`. The scenario holds only the tile layers derived from them (terrain, relief, cover, rivers, which tiles are coast, sea, or ocean) and a handful of hand-written corrections in `data/terrain.json`. `scenarios/age-of-imperialism.json` is generated by `tools/biq-import/` from the community scenario *Age of Imperialism 5.0* (`AoI5.0.biq`, a Civ3 Conquests scenario file that is not included in this repository): its terrain, rivers, roads, rails, mines, irrigation, and natural resources (except the two era markers `Industry` and `Colonialism`), and its nations and cities. Starting units are generated by the importer, not read from the file. The map's author is not credited in the file this was converted from, and the licence for redistributing a conversion has not been confirmed with them: confirm it before publishing the pack. The road and railroad sheets (`terrain/roads.png`, `terrain/railroads.png`, `art/forge/roads.py`) are drawn from scratch; only the layout of the sheets (one cell per combination of the eight neighbours, in Civ3's bit order) was taken from studying the structure of Civ3's road sheets, and no Civ3 art is read or copied. The 43 resource icons (`sprites/resources/`, `art/forge/resources.py`) are drawn from scratch. `scenarios/dawn-straits.json` is the original two-empire starter, converted to the scenario format; its starting units (infantry, cavalry, artillery, pioneers, workers, ironclads) are placed by hand. The worker, farm, and mine sprites come from the same `art/` pipeline as the other unit and relief art, as do the ironclad, transport, battleship, protected cruiser, and torpedo boat (`art/forge/fleet.py`, built in code as 3D puppets and drawn from scratch). Their look and their broadside, sinking and funnel-smoke behaviour were revised after the author looked at the ships of the community scenario *Age of Imperialism 5.0* in the game itself (long low hulls with a tan planked deck over a grey side and a red boot-topping, tall dark oval funnels with trailing smoke, every gun on the beam firing together in a white ring and an orange fireball that turns to brown and black smoke, a magazine explosion and a ring of foam when a ship goes down). That was observation of how they look and move, written down in `docs/combat-animation.md` in our own words; no frame, sprite, palette, sound, or file from that scenario or from Civ3 is read, converted, or copied by the generator or included in the pack. The eleven city skins (`sprites/city_<flavor>.png`, `art/forge/cities.py` and `kit.py`) and the fog and culture-border sheets (`terrain/fog.png`, `terrain/borders.png`, `art/forge/overlay.py`) are likewise drawn in code. Which nation gets which flavor is a judgment recorded in `tools/scenario-forge/data/nations.json`. The fog sheet's layout (9 x 9 diamonds, one per combination of unseen, remembered, and in-sight corners) and the dashed culture-border lines follow how Civ3 and the `open-civ3/` reference present those features (see `open-civ3/reverse-engineering/borders-culture.md`); the pictures themselves are generated from scratch, and no Civ3 art or reference code is included.

The city view is set in two further embedded fonts, both SIL Open Font License 1.1: Playfair Display (`crates/client/fonts/PlayfairDisplay-Variable.ttf`, text in `PlayfairDisplay-OFL.txt`) for names and figures and EB Garamond (`EBGaramond-Variable.ttf`, `EBGaramond-OFL.txt`) for running text. Its parchment, gilt corner bracket, harbour and field engravings and the landscape behind the city's picture (`ui/paper.png`, `ui/flourish.png`, `ui/city_panorama.png`, `ui/city_fields.png`, `ui/city_backdrop.png`) are drawn in code by `art/forge/parchment.py` (`build.py parchment`); the mock-up that guided their layout is not read by the generator.

The client's UI font is the same EB Garamond. The window chrome (`ui/chrome/`: parchment, slate and felt surfaces, gilt frames, studs, cartouches, compass), the yield emblems (`ui/chrome/ico_*.png`) and the terrain vignettes (`ui/chrome/scene_*.png`) are drawn in code by `art/forge/chrome.py`, `art/forge/emblems.py` and `art/forge/scenes.py` (`build.py chrome`). UI glyphs are generated by `art/forge/icons.py`. Game source and generated art use the workspace license declaration (MIT OR Apache-2.0). Two parts of the pack are taken from the Civ3 scenario *Age of Imperialism* by `tools/aoi-art/import.py` (see its docstring), and **their licence for redistribution has not been confirmed**: confirm it, or drop them, before publishing the pack. `nations/<nation id>.png` (200 x 240) are the scenario's leader pictures, which in that scenario are the nations' waving flags (`Art/Leaderheads/*_large.pcx`; for Uruguay, whose picture is not installed, the first frame of `Art/Flics/uruguay.flc`); which picture a nation uses is read from the scenario's own `.biq` and `text/PediaIcons.txt`, so Belgium, Romania and the Boer Republics carry the stand-in flags (Cuba, the Philippines, Ethiopia) that the scenario gives them. The client draws them as the nation's flag in the top bar and as its leader in the diplomacy window. `audio/diplomacy.mp3` is `Sounds/Diplomusic/DipNAEarlyPeace.mp3` of the same scenario, played in a loop under the diplomacy window. Both are optional: a pack without `visuals.nations` and `visuals.diplomacy_music` draws a flag from each nation's colour and plays no music, and nothing else in the project needs Civ3 content to build or run.

The relief/vegetation contexts and their surface-border sheets (`sprites/*_forest.png`,
`sprites/*_jungle.png`, `terrain/*_borders.png`) are generated from original seeded
heightfields and rooted crown shapes in `art/forge/relief.py`. Border masks use that same
surface and projection; no reference images, Civ3 sheets or reference renderer source
enter the generator. The behavior was specified from the terrain notes under
`open-civ3/reverse-engineering/`; the undocumented border-contour algorithm is implemented
independently. The `terrain-study` scenario is a hand-authored visual regression fixture.
